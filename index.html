Presidential Campaign Project Plan

  • Status: Draft v1
  • Date prepared: 2026-08-26
  • Scope: Federal filing, campaign setup, official website requirements, platform development, and first 90-day execution plan.
  • Neutrality note: This is a process and policy-development scaffold. It does not compare, rank, or assess candidates or political parties.
  • Legal note: This is not legal advice. Federal, state, party, ballot-access, tax, employment, and campaign-finance counsel should review before money is raised, spent, or solicited.

Executive Summary

  • Objective: Build a compliant, credible, source-backed path to explore and, if chosen, launch a campaign for President of the United States.
  • Election target assumption: 2028 presidential election cycle.
  • Key legal anchor: A person becomes a federal candidate after raising or spending more than $5,000 for the campaign, unless activity remains within FEC "testing the waters" rules.
  • Key operational anchor: Ballot access is not a single federal filing. Presidential candidates must satisfy state-by-state ballot rules, plus party rules if seeking a party nomination.
  • Key platform anchor: Use "affordability through opportunity" as the organizing economic lane, with proposals scored for cost, labor-market effects, implementation, and distributional impact.

Verified Facts To Track

  • Presidential eligibility:
    • The Constitution requires a President to be a natural born citizen, at least 35 years old, and a U.S. resident for at least 14 years.
    • The 22nd Amendment limits a person to being elected President no more than twice.
  • FEC registration:
    • FEC Form 2 is the Statement of Candidacy and is used for House, Senate, and President.
    • FEC Form 1 is the Statement of Organization and is used to register authorized campaign committees.
    • A candidate must designate a principal campaign committee within 15 days after becoming a candidate.
    • The principal campaign committee must file Form 1 within 10 days after designation.
    • FEC electronic filing is required once total contributions or expenditures exceed, or are expected to exceed, $50,000 in a calendar year.
  • Ballot access:
    • The FEC states that presidential candidates gain ballot access according to state law.
    • The campaign must contact each Secretary of State or appropriate state election office for current requirements.
  • 2025-2026 contribution context:
    • FEC listed individual contributions to candidate committees at $3,500 per election for the 2025-2026 federal election cycle.
    • Contribution limits are adjusted by cycle and must be refreshed for the 2027-2028 cycle.
  • Payroll tax context:
    • For 2026 wages, SSA states OASDI tax is 6.2 percent for employees and 6.2 percent for employers, up to the taxable maximum.
    • SSA states Medicare HI tax is 1.45 percent for employees and 1.45 percent for employers, with no HI taxable maximum after 1993.
  • Federal debt context:
    • CBO's February 2026 outlook projects debt held by the public rising from 101 percent of GDP in 2026 to 120 percent in 2036.
    • CBO projects net interest outlays rising from about $1.0 trillion in 2026 to $2.1 trillion in 2036.

Filing And Legal Formation Workstream

  • Phase 1: Eligibility and candidacy decision
    • Confirm constitutional eligibility with counsel.
    • Confirm no disqualifying legal or state-ballot-access issues.
    • Decide whether current activity is private planning, FEC "testing the waters", or formal candidacy.
    • Create written rules for what staff, volunteers, vendors, and advisors may say before formal launch.
  • Phase 2: Compliance foundation before fundraising
    • Retain campaign-finance counsel.
    • Appoint a qualified treasurer before accepting contributions or making expenditures.
    • Create a compliance calendar for FEC reports, state filings, vendor contracts, contribution screening, and public disclaimers.
    • Select accounting and compliance tools.
    • Create contribution-screening procedures for contribution limits, prohibited sources, foreign nationals, federal contractors, and contributions in another person's name.
    • Create a refund/escalation process for questionable receipts.
  • Phase 3: Committee setup
    • Choose campaign committee name.
    • Obtain EIN.
    • Open campaign bank account after treasurer and counsel review.
    • Draft financial controls for dual approvals, vendor onboarding, reimbursements, contribution processing, record retention, and weekly reconciliation.
  • Phase 4: FEC filing trigger
    • Track all receipts and spending, including testing-the-waters activity that may later need to be reported.
    • If the campaign raises or spends more than $5,000 for candidacy, file FEC Form 2, designate the principal campaign committee, have the committee file FEC Form 1 within 10 days, and begin periodic FEC reporting.
    • If total contributions or expenditures exceed, or are expected to exceed, $50,000 in a calendar year, file electronically and maintain backup e-filing ownership.
  • Phase 5: Ballot access path
    • Choose path: major-party nomination, minor-party nomination, independent candidacy, or write-in strategy where legally available.
    • Build a 56-jurisdiction ballot access matrix covering states, DC, filing authority, filing window, petition signature requirement, elector slate requirement, filing fee, notary/witness rules, challenge period, withdrawal/substitution rules, source URL, and responsible owner.
    • Refresh the matrix monthly until all 2028 deadlines are final.

Official Campaign Website Workstream

  • Purpose:
    • Provide a public source of record for the campaign.
    • Collect compliant donations.
    • Recruit volunteers.
    • Publish platform material.
    • Support press verification.
    • Maintain public-facing disclaimers and privacy terms.
  • Core pages:
    • Home: candidate name, office/year, purpose statement, donate action, volunteer action, email/SMS signup, latest public update.
    • About: biography, qualifications, public-service or leadership record, privacy-reviewed personal details.
    • Platform: issue pillars, short summaries, source-backed policy briefs, downloadable PDF version.
    • Filing and transparency: committee name, FEC ID after filing, public FEC profile link after available.
    • Donate: contribution limits, eligibility attestation, employer/occupation collection where required, refund policy, recurring donation controls, clear FEC disclaimer.
    • Volunteer: signup form, state, availability, skill area, opt-in consent.
    • Events: public calendar, RSVP, accessibility notes, press access rules.
    • Newsroom: press releases, speeches, media contact, downloadable headshots/logos after brand approval.
    • Contact: general inbox, press inbox, compliance/legal intake, mailing address after counsel review.
    • Privacy, terms, accessibility, and SMS/email opt-out.
  • Compliance requirements:
    • Include clear paid-for disclaimers on campaign public communications.
    • For internet public communications with text or graphics, FEC says the written disclaimer should be viewable without action, clearly readable, and have reasonable color contrast unless an adapted disclaimer applies.
    • Put a persistent footer disclaimer on every page.
    • Have counsel review donation page language, volunteer intake, SMS consent, and privacy terms before launch.
  • Website quality requirements:
    • Mobile-first layout.
    • WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility target.
    • HTTPS, HSTS, secure DNS, MFA on all admin accounts.
    • No public secrets or unmanaged tracking tags.
    • Claim-review workflow for every issue statement.
    • Versioned platform pages so edits are traceable.
    • Analytics configured to protect privacy and comply with consent requirements.

Platform Development Workstream

  • Operating rule:
    • Treat each platform pillar as a policy product with a problem statement, affected groups, current law, historical precedents, options, cost estimate, implementation path, constitutional/federalism review, risk analysis, and measurable outcomes.

Pillar 1: Empowerment Through Economic Opportunity

  • User-stated message boundary:
    • Focus on affordability, not freebies.
    • Focus on hiring, work, skills, housing, energy, food, transportation, and family formation costs.
    • Move beyond "buy American, hire American" into measurable incentives for entry-level opportunity.
  • Historical initiative examples to study:
    • Inflation Reduction Act: clean-energy tax credits, energy community bonus credits, low-income community energy investment incentives, health and household-cost provisions, and deficit effects subject to current-law review.
    • Qualified Opportunity Zones: tax incentives intended to spur economic growth and job creation in low-income communities; evaluate investment quality, job creation, displacement, transparency, and community benefit.
    • CHIPS and Science Act: federal semiconductor and R&D investment; workforce, supply-chain, and manufacturing capacity lessons.
    • Bipartisan Infrastructure Law / Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act: roads, bridges, broadband, water, safety, and infrastructure workforce lessons.
    • GI Bill: education and training benefit model tied to national service.
    • Pell Grants and Title I: need-based education support and low-income school funding models.
  • Youth employer-side FICA proposal:
    • Draft working concept: no employer-side FICA on eligible employees ages 16 through 25.
    • Employee-side FICA remains unchanged unless separately proposed.
    • Employer relief is framed as a hiring-cost reduction, not a direct cash grant to individuals.
    • Core design questions:
      • Does the exemption cover OASDI, Medicare HI, or both?
      • Does eligibility end on the worker's 26th birthday or at the end of the calendar year?
      • Does the policy apply to all wages or only wages up to a cap?
      • Does it apply to all eligible workers or only incremental/new hires?
      • Are internships, apprenticeships, part-time jobs, seasonal jobs, and gig work included?
      • How are Social Security and Medicare trust funds backfilled?
      • Is the exemption temporary, permanent, or triggered by youth unemployment rates?
    • Anti-abuse safeguards to evaluate:
      • no displacement of older workers,
      • no reclassification of contractors to avoid rules,
      • wage floor or hours protections,
      • apprenticeship/training documentation,
      • penalties for age-based churn,
      • small-business safe harbor,
      • audit trail through payroll filings.
    • Required analysis:
      • revenue score,
      • effect on youth hiring,
      • effect on wages,
      • distribution by firm size and industry,
      • interaction with Social Security and Medicare financing,
      • state payroll-tax and unemployment-insurance interactions.

Pillar 2: Equality And Accountability In Law Enforcement

  • User-stated message boundary:
    • "Defund the police" is not the debate frame.
    • Develop a public-safety platform that treats accountability and effective policing as compatible goals.
  • Historical initiative examples to study:
    • President's Task Force on 21st Century Policing: trust, legitimacy, policy, oversight, technology, social media, community policing, training, education, officer wellness, and safety.
    • DOJ pattern-or-practice investigations and consent decrees: federal accountability tools for systemic misconduct.
    • First Step Act: federal criminal-justice reform and implementation lessons.
  • Policy modules:
    • national use-of-force and misconduct data standards,
    • accreditation tied to federal grants,
    • independent investigation protocols for serious incidents,
    • body-camera and evidence-retention standards,
    • recruitment, retention, and officer wellness,
    • de-escalation and crisis-response training,
    • mental-health co-response and diversion,
    • community trust metrics,
    • due-process protections for civilians and officers.

Pillar 3: Equip And Empower The Military, Teachers, Students, And Allies

  • Military:
    • Readiness, housing, healthcare, mental health, family support, cyber, AI, logistics, industrial-base resilience, and transition-to-civilian career paths.
  • Teachers:
    • Teacher pipeline, compensation structure, classroom safety, apprenticeship/residency models, credential reciprocity, and professional support.
  • Students:
    • Early literacy/numeracy, career pathways, apprenticeships, community college, Pell access, skills credentials, and AI-era learning tools.
  • Allies:
    • Interoperability, cyber defense, defense industrial capacity, supply-chain resilience, energy security, and treaty commitments.

Pillar 4: Reduce The Federal Budget's Reliance On Debt

  • Working frame:
    • Elevate the debt burden as a governing issue without presenting debt reduction as an isolated austerity exercise.
    • Pair fiscal discipline with growth, workforce participation, productivity, and implementation reform.
  • Policy modules:
    • transparent debt and interest dashboard,
    • tax expenditure review,
    • program performance review,
    • emergency-spending rules,
    • multi-year budget targets,
    • faster permitting and productivity investments,
    • fraud/waste reduction,
    • entitlement solvency options for public debate,
    • defense and non-defense procurement reform,
    • revenue options scored by distribution and growth impact.
  • Required analysis:
    • CBO baseline,
    • trust-fund effects,
    • debt-to-GDP path,
    • net interest path,
    • distributional effects,
    • implementation risk,
    • political feasibility,
    • legal authority.

First 90 Days

  • Days 1-15:
    • Confirm eligibility and personal readiness.
    • Retain campaign-finance counsel.
    • Identify treasurer candidates.
    • Create testing-the-waters rules.
    • Set up secure workspace, password manager, MFA, and document repository.
    • Start source-backed platform research memos.
  • Days 16-30:
    • Choose committee naming options.
    • Draft financial controls.
    • Select compliance/accounting software.
    • Build the ballot access matrix template.
    • Create official website requirements document.
    • Draft platform pillar one-page briefs.
  • Days 31-60:
    • Finalize treasurer and counsel roles.
    • Prepare FEC Form 2 and Form 1 data in advance of trigger.
    • Build website prototype.
    • Draft donation workflow with compliance review.
    • Create press biography, issue brief template, and claims-review process.
    • Commission initial scoring work for employer-side youth FICA proposal.
  • Days 61-90:
    • Decide formal launch timing.
    • File FEC documents if threshold/launch decision requires filing.
    • Launch compliant website only after legal review.
    • Assign state ballot access owners.
    • Publish initial platform briefs after sourcing and review.
    • Start weekly compliance, policy, operations, and security review cadence.

Risk Register

  • High impact:
    • Ballot-access deadlines or signature rules are missed.
    • Campaign raises or spends above FEC threshold without timely filing.
    • Contribution screening fails.
    • Website donation flow launches before compliance approval.
    • Platform claims are published without source review.
  • Medium impact:
    • Youth FICA proposal creates Social Security/Medicare trust-fund questions without a backfill model.
    • Law-enforcement platform is interpreted as either anti-police or anti-accountability because definitions are vague.
    • Debt platform lacks specific baseline and scoring assumptions.
    • Education/military/allies pillar becomes too broad without measurable outcomes.
  • Watch items:
    • 2027-2028 contribution limits are not yet reflected in this draft.
    • 2028 state ballot-access calendars may not be final as of 2026-08-26.
    • Campaign website privacy/SMS rules require counsel and vendor review.
